01

RECONNAISSANCE // EXPERIMENTAL FLEET

Er'katri “Katri” Kinastra

Admiral · Task Force Delta

Katri's priority is to understand what the enemy is doing before committing the Federation to a costly response. His decision to investigate behind enemy lines exposes the EOA's involvement and gives the Council a stronger basis for action.

Operational perspective

  • Questions assumptions and seeks evidence that can distinguish an immediate threat from a politically convenient explanation.
  • Favors reconnaissance, adaptation, and controlled use of experimental assets rather than destruction without a clear purpose.
  • Recognizes that the Dreadnought's deployment may be necessary, while understanding that its use could raise the stakes of the conflict.
“Understand the unknown. Adapt to it. Survive it. Then find the way home.”
02

ORBITAL COMMAND // FLEET COORDINATION

Reina Flynn

Senator Reina · Senior Fleet Commander

Reina approaches the crisis through the strategic map: orbital control, supply routes, fleet positioning, and the infrastructure required to keep an operation from collapsing into a prolonged disaster.

Operational perspective

  • Prioritizes orbital superiority and the protection of logistics infrastructure needed by both military forces and civilians.
  • Prefers to disable an enemy's ability to coordinate and reinforce rather than destroy assets that could be secured or repurposed.
  • Understands the Senate's concerns, but sees delay as dangerous once the EOA's wider intentions are confirmed.
Control the routes, preserve the infrastructure, and the fleet can keep the situation from becoming unmanageable.
03

GROUND OPERATIONS // MEDICAL SUPPORT

Eira Snowstorm

Combat Medic

Eira measures the operation by what happens to the people caught inside it. For her, a secured objective means little if evacuation routes are cut, medical access is lost, or civilians are treated as an acceptable cost.

Operational perspective

  • Prioritizes civilian protection, evacuation corridors, triage, and reliable access for medical teams.
  • Supports neutralizing immediate threats but opposes unnecessary destruction that makes the aftermath harder to survive.
  • Sees the Senate's caution as a necessary reminder that the Federation's legitimacy depends on how it treats people during a crisis.
A position is not truly secure if the people living there have nowhere safe to go.
